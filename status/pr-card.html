PERMANENT RESIDENT CARD

Your PR Card Is Proof of Your Existing Permanent Resident Status

A Permanent Resident Card, commonly called a PR card, is an official document that shows that you have permanent resident status in Canada.

Renewing a PR card is not the same as applying for permanent residence again. You already hold permanent resident status. The application is for a new document confirming that status.

A PR card is particularly important when travelling outside Canada. Permanent residents generally need a valid PR card, together with a valid passport or travel document, to return to Canada on a commercial carrier such as an airplane, train, bus or boat.

A PR card application may also involve an assessment of whether you continue to meet Canada's permanent resident residency obligation.

PR CARD VS. PR STATUS

An Expired PR Card Does Not Mean Your PR Status Has Expired

Your permanent resident card and your permanent resident status are two different things.

The PR card is a physical document used to prove your status. The expiry date printed on the card is the expiry date of the document, not automatically the end of your permanent resident status.

If your PR card expires while you are living in Canada, you do not automatically become a temporary resident or lose the right to remain in Canada.

Permanent resident status can only be lost through specific legal processes, such as a formal determination that you have lost status, voluntary renunciation, an enforceable removal order or becoming a Canadian citizen.

Expired Card ≠ Expired PR Status

Renewing your PR card replaces the document. It does not mean that you are applying to become a permanent resident again.

WHEN TO APPLY

When Should You Renew Your PR Card?

You generally do not need to wait until your PR card has already expired before applying for a new one.

01

Expiring Within 9 Months

You can generally apply to renew your current PR card when it will expire in less than nine months.

02

Already Expired

If your PR card has already expired, you can apply for a new card while you continue to hold permanent resident status.

03

Lost, Stolen or Destroyed

You can apply to replace a PR card that has been lost, stolen or destroyed.

04

Damaged Card

A PR card that is physically damaged or whose chip is damaged may need to be replaced.

05

Name or Information Change

You may need a new PR card when your legal name or other information shown on the card has changed.

06

Never Received Your Card

Different procedures may apply if your PR card was issued but never received.

RENEWAL TIMING

Do Not Normally Renew More Than 9 Months Before Expiry

IRCC generally instructs permanent residents not to submit a renewal application while their current PR card is still valid for more than nine months.

Applications submitted too early may be returned unless a specific exception applies, such as a legal name change.

If you have upcoming travel, the expiry date on your card should be reviewed well in advance.

RESIDENCY OBLIGATION

Do You Meet the Permanent Resident Residency Obligation?

A PR card renewal may require IRCC to review whether you have maintained your permanent resident residency obligation.

01

730 Days

Permanent residents generally need at least 730 qualifying days within the applicable five-year period.

02

Days Do Not Need to Be Continuous

The required 730 days do not need to be accumulated consecutively.

03

Physical Presence in Canada

Days physically spent in Canada generally count toward the residency obligation.

04

Some Time Abroad May Count

Certain periods spent outside Canada may count toward the residency obligation when specific legal requirements are met.

NEWER PERMANENT RESIDENTS

Have You Been a Permanent Resident for Less Than 5 Years?

The residency obligation is not calculated in exactly the same way for every permanent resident.

If you have been a permanent resident for five years or more, IRCC generally looks at the five-year period immediately before the assessment.

If you became a permanent resident less than five years ago, the question is generally whether you will still be able to accumulate at least 730 qualifying days within the first five years after becoming a permanent resident.

Don't Simply Count Back 5 Years in Every Case

The correct residency calculation depends on how long you have already held permanent resident status.

TIME OUTSIDE CANADA

When Can Time Outside Canada Count?

Certain time spent abroad may count toward the 730-day residency requirement if the applicable conditions are satisfied.

01

Accompanying a Canadian Citizen

Time abroad may count when you are accompanying your Canadian citizen spouse or common-law partner, or, in certain cases, a Canadian citizen parent.

02

Canadian Business Employment

Certain full-time employment outside Canada for a qualifying Canadian business or public administration may count.

03

Accompanying a PR Working Abroad

Certain time may count when accompanying a permanent resident spouse, partner or parent who is qualifying through full-time employment with a Canadian business or public administration.

TRAVEL HISTORY

Accurate Travel History Is an Important Part of the Application

A PR card application asks for information about absences from Canada during the relevant period.

Applicants should carefully review passports, travel records and other documentation when reconstructing their travel history.

Missing or inaccurate travel dates can become particularly important where the applicant is close to the minimum residency requirement.

Close to 730 Days?

When residency days are close to the minimum, even small errors in travel calculations can be significant.

RESIDENCY CONCERNS

What If You Do Not Appear to Have 730 Qualifying Days?

A residency shortfall should be reviewed carefully before submitting a routine PR card renewal application.

Not every day outside Canada is necessarily excluded from the residency calculation, and the correct five-year assessment period must first be established.

Where the residency obligation is not met, humanitarian and compassionate circumstances may also be relevant to IRCC's assessment depending on the facts of the case.

A PR card expiry by itself does not terminate permanent resident status. Even a person who may not meet the residency obligation remains a permanent resident until a formal status determination is made.

Do Not Treat This as a Routine Renewal

If you have spent substantial time outside Canada, have unusual family circumstances or are unsure whether you meet the residency obligation, consider reviewing the case before applying.

INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL

Travelling With a PR Card

Your PR card is especially important when returning to Canada after international travel.

01

Air Travel

A permanent resident travelling to Canada by commercial airline generally needs a valid PR card or Permanent Resident Travel Document.

02

Commercial Bus, Train or Boat

The valid PR card requirement also generally applies when travelling to Canada by commercial bus, train or boat.

03

Private Vehicle

A permanent resident travelling to Canada by land in a private vehicle does not have the same PR card or PRTD boarding requirement that applies to commercial carriers. They must still establish their identity and permanent resident status when examined at the Canadian border.

OUTSIDE CANADA

You Cannot Normally Apply for a PR Card From Outside Canada

To be eligible to receive a PR card, you must generally be in Canada.

IRCC mails PR cards only to Canadian addresses and does not mail PR cards outside Canada.

If you are already outside Canada without a valid PR card and need to return on a commercial carrier, the appropriate application is generally a Permanent Resident Travel Document rather than a PR card renewal from abroad.

Outside Canada Without a Valid PR Card?

You may need a Permanent Resident Travel Document to return to Canada before applying for a new PR card.

PERMANENT RESIDENT TRAVEL DOCUMENT

What Is a PRTD?

A Permanent Resident Travel Document, commonly called a PRTD, is a temporary travel document for certain permanent residents who are outside Canada without a valid PR card.

It can allow an eligible permanent resident to board a commercial carrier and travel back to Canada.

A PRTD may be issued for a single entry or, in appropriate circumstances, for multiple entries. After returning to Canada, a permanent resident without a valid PR card should generally apply for a new PR card.

PR Card and PRTD Are Different Documents

A PR card is generally obtained while you are in Canada. A PRTD is generally used when you are outside Canada without a valid PR card and need to return.

URGENT PROCESSING

Need Your PR Card Urgently?

IRCC provides urgent PR card processing in certain qualifying circumstances.

Applicants requesting urgent processing must provide evidence supporting the reason for urgency and their travel plans.

Even when an applicant qualifies for urgent processing, IRCC does not guarantee that the new PR card will be issued before the intended travel date.

Current IRCC guidance distinguishes between travel that is more than three weeks away and travel within the next three weeks. IRCC does not guarantee urgent processing and states that it cannot process a PR card application in less than three weeks. A person who leaves Canada without a valid PR card may need to apply for a PRTD from outside Canada to return by a commercial carrier.

Upcoming Travel?

If you have urgent travel and your PR card is expired or expiring, review both urgent processing and PRTD implications before making travel arrangements.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

Important Things to Know About PR Cards

PR card validity and permanent resident status are often confused.

!

My Card Expired, So I Lost PR

No. An expired PR card does not by itself terminate permanent resident status.

!

Renewal Means Applying for PR Again

No. A PR card renewal is an application for a new proof-of-status document, not a new permanent residence application.

!

The Card Must Always Be Valid in Canada

An expired card does not automatically prevent a permanent resident from continuing to live in Canada.

!

PR Card Expiry Determines Residency

The expiry date printed on the card is not the same thing as the legal residency-obligation calculation.

!

Every Day Abroad Is Lost

Certain time outside Canada can count toward the residency obligation if specific requirements are met.

!

I Can Renew From Overseas

PR card applications are generally made while the permanent resident is in Canada. A PRTD may be required to return from abroad.
