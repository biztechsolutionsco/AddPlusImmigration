RESTORATION

What Is Restoration of Temporary Status?

Restoration is a process that may allow certain visitors, workers or students who have lost temporary resident status in Canada to ask IRCC to restore that status.

Under the general rules, restoration is normally available only for a limited period after status has been lost.

Restoration is different from an extension. An extension is generally submitted while your current temporary status is still valid, while restoration applies after status has already been lost.

Approval is discretionary and is never guaranteed.

GENERAL RESTORATION PERIOD

The 90-Day Deadline Is Critical

Under Canada's general restoration rules, an eligible temporary resident normally must apply within 90 days after losing status.

The restoration application must be received within the applicable deadline.

If more than 90 days have passed and no special policy applies, the person generally cannot use the normal restoration process and may need to leave Canada.

Certain temporary public policies or special measures can create exceptions, so current IRCC instructions should be checked before concluding that restoration is unavailable.

Less Than 90 Days?

Do not delay. Restoration deadlines are time-sensitive, and approval is not automatic.

ELIGIBILITY

Who May Be Eligible for Restoration?

Restoration depends on why status was lost, whether the application is timely and whether the person continues to meet the applicable requirements.

01

Lost Temporary Status

You must have previously held temporary resident status in Canada as a visitor, worker or student.

02

Apply Within the Deadline

Under the general rules, the restoration request must normally be made within 90 days of losing status.

03

Continue to Meet Requirements

You must continue to satisfy the applicable requirements for the temporary status or permit being requested.

04

Previous Conditions Matter

IRCC considers whether you complied with the conditions imposed on your previous temporary status.

05

Explain What Happened

A restoration application should clearly explain the circumstances that resulted in the loss of status.

06

Officer Approval Required

Meeting the basic requirements allows you to request restoration but does not guarantee that IRCC will approve it.

HOW STATUS MAY BE LOST

Common Restoration Situations

Restoration may arise from an expired document or certain failures to comply with the conditions of temporary status.

01

Status Expired

Your authorized stay ended before you submitted an application to extend or change your status.

02

Work Permit Conditions

A worker may have changed employer, work location or occupation before obtaining the required new authorization.

03

Study Permit Conditions

A student may have changed an educational institution or another condition that required prior immigration authorization.

04

Missed Extension Deadline

An extension application submitted only after the previous permit expired generally does not create maintained status.

05

Family Members

If several accompanying family members lose their status, each person generally needs their own restoration application.

06

Other Status Problems

Different facts can lead to loss of status, so the exact immigration history should be reviewed before selecting the application.

VISITORS

Restoring Status as a Visitor

A person who remained in Canada beyond their authorized visitor status may be able to apply to restore visitor status if they remain eligible and apply within the applicable deadline.

The application should explain why the applicant remained in Canada after losing status and why they wish to continue staying temporarily in Canada.

The applicant must continue to satisfy Canada's temporary resident requirements.

Visitor Status Expired?

Under the general rules, restoration may still be possible if fewer than 90 days have passed since visitor status expired.

Visitor Status Information

WORKERS

Restoring Worker Status

If your work permit expires before you properly apply to extend or change it, you generally lose worker status.

You must stop working when you lose the authorization provided by your work permit.

If you remain eligible, you may be able to apply within the general restoration period to restore your temporary status and obtain a new work permit.

The new work permit requirements still apply, including any employer-specific, LMIA or LMIA-exempt requirements relevant to the position.

Stop Working After Status Is Lost

Filing a restoration and work permit application generally does not authorize you to continue working while IRCC processes it.

Work Permit Information

STUDENTS

Restoring Student Status

A student loses student status if their study permit expires before they submit the appropriate extension application.

Students can also lose status by failing to comply with certain conditions attached to their study permit.

If eligible, the student may apply within the applicable restoration period to restore status and obtain a new study permit.

The student generally cannot continue or restart studies until IRCC restores their student status and issues the new study permit.

Do Not Continue Studying

A restoration application does not provide the same study authorization as maintained status.

WHILE WAITING

Restoration Is Not Maintained Status

This is one of the most important differences between filing before and after your status expires.

M

R

!

Students

CHANGING YOUR TEMPORARY STATUS

Do You Have to Restore the Same Type of Status?

The correct strategy depends on what temporary status you previously held and what immigration authorization you now require.

For example, a person who no longer needs or no longer qualifies for a work or study permit may in some circumstances seek to restore temporary resident status as a visitor.

However, changing the requested status does not remove the need to satisfy restoration requirements and the eligibility requirements for the new temporary status.

Worker, Student or Visitor?

The appropriate restoration application depends on both your previous status and what you intend to do next in Canada.

MORE THAN 90 DAYS?

What If the General Restoration Period Has Passed?

Under the normal restoration rules, a person who has been out of status for more than 90 days generally cannot submit a regular restoration application.

Depending on the circumstances, the person may need to leave Canada and apply for the required immigration authorization from outside Canada.

However, temporary public policies and special measures sometimes create exceptions for particular groups or circumstances.

Current IRCC instructions should therefore be checked before deciding that no restoration option exists.

Special Measures May Exist

Certain temporary public policies can modify the normal restoration deadline for eligible applicants. These measures change over time.

TEMPORARY SPECIAL MEASURES

Some Applicants May Have Extra Time

Canada sometimes introduces temporary immigration measures for people affected by emergencies or other exceptional circumstances.

For example, current measures for certain temporary residents directly affected by qualifying natural disasters in Canada can provide more time than the normal 90-day restoration period.

These policies have specific eligibility dates and conditions and should not be assumed to apply to every out-of-status person.

Check Current IRCC Measures

Temporary public policies can begin, change or expire. Always verify the current rules for your circumstances.

APPLICATION DEADLINE

Online Deadlines Use UTC

IRCC's online application system uses Coordinated Universal Time, or UTC, when determining whether certain restoration applications were received within the applicable deadline.

This means midnight in your local Canadian time zone may not be the actual online submission deadline.

Applicants should avoid waiting until the final day of the restoration period.

Submit Early

A missed restoration deadline can have significant immigration consequences.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Restoration Does Not Erase the Period You Were Out of Status

Restoration is a request asking IRCC to restore temporary resident status after it has been lost.

It should not be confused with maintained status, where a timely application allows the previous status to continue while a decision is pending.

The applicant should accurately disclose the circumstances surrounding the loss of status and avoid unauthorized work or study while waiting for restoration.

The underlying eligibility requirements for the requested visitor, work or study status must also continue to be met.
