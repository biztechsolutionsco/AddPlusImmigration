PERMANENT RESIDENT TRAVEL DOCUMENT

Returning to Canada Without a Valid PR Card

A Permanent Resident Travel Document, commonly called a PRTD, is a travel document for Canadian permanent residents who are outside Canada and do not have a valid PR card.

It is generally used when a permanent resident needs to board a commercial carrier such as an airplane, bus, train or boat to return to Canada.

A PRTD does not grant permanent resident status. The applicant must already be a Canadian permanent resident.

It is also not a replacement for renewing a PR card. Once the permanent resident returns to Canada, they should generally apply for a new PR card if they do not have a valid one.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A PRTD Does Not Give You Permanent Resident Status

A PRTD is available to people who already hold Canadian permanent resident status.

It is simply a travel document that can help an eligible permanent resident return to Canada when they do not have a valid PR card.

It is therefore completely different from applying for permanent residence through Express Entry, family sponsorship, a provincial nominee program or another immigration pathway.

PRTD = Travel Document

You must already be a permanent resident before you can qualify for a PRTD.

ELIGIBILITY

Who Can Apply for a PRTD?

A PRTD is intended for permanent residents outside Canada who need documentary proof to return on a commercial carrier.

01

You Are a Permanent Resident

You must currently have permanent resident status in Canada.

02

You Are Outside Canada

A PRTD application is made while you are physically outside Canada.

03

You Do Not Have a Valid PR Card

Your PR card may be expired, lost, stolen, damaged or otherwise unavailable.

04

You Need to Return Commercially

You intend to return to Canada by airplane, commercial bus, train or boat.

COMMON SITUATIONS

When Might You Need a PRTD?

Permanent residents can unexpectedly find themselves outside Canada without valid PR card documentation.

01

PR Card Expired Abroad

You travelled outside Canada and your PR card expired before you were ready to return.

02

PR Card Lost or Stolen

Your valid PR card was lost or stolen while you were outside Canada.

03

PR Card Damaged

Your card became damaged and can no longer be used for commercial travel.

04

New PR Card Not Ready Before Travel

You applied for a new PR card in Canada but left before the card was issued or received.

COMMERCIAL TRAVEL

Why Do Permanent Residents Need a PRTD to Board?

When travelling to Canada on a commercial carrier, permanent residents generally need acceptable documentation showing that they are authorized to enter Canada as permanent residents.

A valid PR card normally serves this purpose.

If no valid PR card is available, a PRTD can provide the travel documentation needed to board an airplane, bus, boat or train to Canada.

Airline Will Not Accept an Expired PR Card

An expired PR card does not normally provide the documentation needed to board a commercial flight to Canada.

PRIVATE VEHICLES

Returning to Canada by Private Vehicle Is Different

The commercial-carrier documentation requirement is different from entering Canada at a land border in a private vehicle.

A private vehicle may include a car, truck, motorcycle or recreational vehicle that you own, borrow or rent.

Permanent residents travelling in a private vehicle may be able to establish their permanent resident status using other documentation at the Canadian border.

Commercial vs. Private Travel Matters

Whether you need a PRTD can depend on how you intend to travel back to Canada.

RESIDENCY OBLIGATION

IRCC Will Review Whether You Have Maintained PR Status

A PRTD application is not simply a travel request. IRCC may assess whether the permanent resident meets Canada's residency obligation.

01

730 Qualifying Days

Permanent residents generally need at least 730 qualifying days during the applicable five-year period.

02

Days Do Not Need to Be Continuous

Qualifying days can be accumulated at different times and do not need to be consecutive.

03

Some Time Outside Canada May Count

Certain periods abroad may count toward the residency obligation where specific legal requirements are met.

04

Travel History Matters

The applicant must provide accurate information about absences from Canada during the relevant period.

NEWER PERMANENT RESIDENTS

Have You Been a Permanent Resident for Less Than Five Years?

The residency assessment differs depending on how long the person has held permanent resident status.

If you have been a permanent resident for less than five years, IRCC generally considers whether you will still be able to accumulate at least 730 qualifying days by the fifth anniversary of becoming a permanent resident.

This is different from simply counting 730 days during the period immediately before the PRTD application.

The Correct Five-Year Period Matters

Residency calculations should be completed carefully before the application is submitted.

QUALIFYING TIME ABROAD

Can Time Outside Canada Count Toward 730 Days?

In specific circumstances, time spent outside Canada can count toward the permanent resident residency obligation.

01

Accompanying a Canadian Citizen

Certain time abroad may count when accompanying a Canadian citizen spouse or common-law partner or, in some situations, a Canadian citizen parent.

02

Employment Outside Canada

Certain full-time employment abroad for a qualifying Canadian business or Canadian public administration may count.

03

Accompanying a PR Working Abroad

Certain periods may count when accompanying a permanent resident spouse, partner or parent who qualifies through eligible Canadian employment abroad.

RESIDENCY CONCERNS

What If You Do Not Meet the 730-Day Requirement?

A PRTD application can have significant consequences when the applicant does not meet the permanent resident residency obligation.

Before assuming that the requirement has not been met, the residency period, physical presence and any qualifying time outside Canada should be reviewed carefully.

If a residency shortfall remains, humanitarian and compassionate considerations may be relevant depending on the person's circumstances.

A negative residency determination may affect the person's permanent resident status.

Do Not Treat This as a Routine Travel Application

If you have spent substantial time outside Canada, review your residency obligation before submitting a PRTD application.

HUMANITARIAN & COMPASSIONATE CONSIDERATIONS

Exceptional Circumstances May Be Relevant

Where a permanent resident does not meet the residency obligation, IRCC may consider humanitarian and compassionate circumstances in determining whether permanent resident status should nevertheless be retained.

Relevant circumstances depend on the individual case and may involve family, medical, child-related or other compelling factors.

These applications should clearly explain what prevented compliance with the residency obligation and provide supporting evidence for the circumstances relied upon.

H&C Is Case-Specific

Humanitarian considerations are not an automatic exemption from the residency obligation. The complete circumstances and supporting evidence must be assessed.

PRTD VALIDITY

A PRTD Is Normally Issued for One Entry to Canada

A Permanent Resident Travel Document is generally intended to allow a permanent resident to return to Canada.

Most PRTDs are issued for a single entry. Once the permanent resident returns to Canada, the person should generally obtain a valid PR card before travelling again.

In certain circumstances, IRCC may consider issuing a multiple-entry PRTD to a permanent resident who meets the residency obligation and demonstrates a need for repeated travel.

Multiple Entry May Be Possible

Applicants requesting a multiple-entry PRTD can explain their circumstances and request consideration, but approval is discretionary.

AFTER RETURNING TO CANADA

Apply for a New PR Card After You Return

A PRTD is not intended to replace a PR card on an ongoing basis.

After returning to Canada, a permanent resident without a valid PR card should generally apply to renew or replace the card.

PR cards are issued to permanent residents in Canada and are not mailed outside Canada.

The record must generally show entries to and exits from China covering the five years before the PRTD application.

The document must also be translated into English or French where required.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

Important Things to Know About PRTDs

PRTDs are often confused with PR cards and permanent residence applications.

!

A PRTD Gives Me PR Status

No. You must already hold permanent resident status to qualify.

!

A PRTD Is a PR Card

No. It is a travel document used primarily to return to Canada when a valid PR card is unavailable.

!

My PR Card Expired, So I Lost PR

An expired PR card does not by itself terminate permanent resident status.

!

I Can Apply From Inside Canada

No. A PRTD is for permanent residents who are outside Canada.

!

Every PRTD Is Multiple Entry

No. A PRTD is normally issued for a single entry, although multiple-entry issuance may be considered in certain cases.

!

Residency Does Not Matter

IRCC can assess compliance with the permanent resident residency obligation when deciding a PRTD application.
