LMIA FOR EMPLOYERS

Hiring a Temporary Foreign Worker in Canada

A Labour Market Impact Assessment, commonly known as an LMIA, is an assessment completed by Employment and Social Development Canada and Service Canada.

In many situations, a Canadian employer must obtain a positive LMIA before a foreign worker can apply for an employer-specific work permit.

The LMIA process examines the proposed employment, wage, recruitment efforts, working conditions and the potential impact of hiring a temporary foreign worker on Canada's labour market.

Employers must meet detailed program requirements and demonstrate that the position and recruitment efforts comply with the applicable Temporary Foreign Worker Program stream.

WHAT IS AN LMIA?

What Does the Government Assess?

The LMIA process helps determine whether hiring a foreign worker is justified under Canada's temporary foreign worker rules.

01

Genuine Labour Need

The employer must demonstrate a genuine need to fill the position and meet applicable program requirements.

02

Recruitment Efforts

Employers generally must recruit Canadians and permanent residents before seeking to hire a foreign worker.

03

Wage

The offered wage must meet the applicable wage requirements for the occupation and LMIA stream.

04

Working Conditions

Employment conditions must meet applicable federal, provincial and territorial employment standards.

05

Employer Compliance

The employer must meet Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements and comply with applicable immigration and employment rules.

06

Labour Market Impact

Service Canada assesses whether hiring the foreign worker is likely to have a positive or neutral impact on the Canadian labour market.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

An LMIA Is Not the Foreign Worker's Work Permit

The LMIA application is made by the Canadian employer to Employment and Social Development Canada and Service Canada.

If the employer receives a positive LMIA, the employer can provide the foreign worker with the required documents to support an employer-specific work permit application.

The worker then applies separately to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada for permission to work in Canada.

Employer First, Worker Second

In an LMIA-required case, the employer generally completes the LMIA process first. The foreign worker then uses the positive LMIA to support their work permit application.

LMIA STREAMS

Choosing the Correct LMIA Stream

The correct application stream depends on factors such as the wage, occupation, industry, location and purpose of the hire.

01

High-Wage Positions

Positions offered at or above the applicable provincial or territorial wage threshold are generally processed under the high-wage stream.

02

Low-Wage Positions

Positions offered below the applicable wage threshold are generally processed under the low-wage stream and are subject to additional program restrictions.

03

Global Talent Stream

Certain highly skilled occupations and qualifying employers may be eligible for the Global Talent Stream.

04

Agricultural Workers

Employers hiring workers in primary agriculture may use specialized agricultural LMIA streams.

05

In-Home Caregivers

Certain caregiver positions may be processed under specific Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements.

06

Permanent Residence Support

Employers may also use certain LMIA processes to support skilled workers in connection with permanent residence.

HIGH-WAGE OR LOW-WAGE

The Wage Threshold Helps Determine the Stream

Employers must compare the wage offered for the position with the applicable provincial or territorial wage threshold.

Positions at or above the threshold are generally processed through the high-wage stream.

Positions below the threshold generally fall under the low-wage stream and may face additional restrictions relating to the proportion of temporary foreign workers at the work location and other program requirements.

Wage Determines More Than Salary

The wage can affect the LMIA stream, recruitment requirements and employer obligations.

RECRUITMENT

Employers Generally Must Recruit in Canada First

Recruitment is one of the central parts of many LMIA applications.

01

Recruit Canadians and Permanent Residents

Employers generally must make genuine efforts to recruit qualified Canadian citizens and permanent residents.

02

Job Bank

Job Bank advertising is commonly required, subject to the rules of the applicable LMIA stream.

03

Additional Recruitment Methods

Employers may also need to advertise through additional recruitment methods that are appropriate for the occupation.

04

Advertising Period

Recruitment must generally be conducted for the required period and at the appropriate time before the LMIA application is submitted.

05

Recruitment Records

Employers should retain evidence of advertisements, applicants and recruitment results.

06

Explain Recruitment Results

The employer may need to explain why Canadian or permanent resident applicants were not hired.

WAGES

The Offered Wage Must Meet Program Requirements

The wage offered to the temporary foreign worker must comply with the requirements of the applicable LMIA stream.

Employers should review the prevailing wage for the occupation and region and ensure that the employment offer is consistent with Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements.

Offering a lower wage to a foreign worker simply because they require a work permit is not acceptable.

Wage Errors Can Affect the Entire Application

The occupation, wage and location should be reviewed together before recruitment begins.

HIGH-WAGE LMIA

High-Wage Positions

Employers hiring temporary foreign workers in high-wage positions must meet the requirements of the high-wage stream.

01

Wage Threshold

The offered wage must fall within the high-wage classification based on the current provincial or territorial threshold.

02

Recruitment

Employers must meet the applicable high-wage recruitment and advertising requirements.

03

Transition Plan

In many high-wage applications, employers must prepare a transition plan describing steps to reduce reliance on temporary foreign workers.

04

Employer Compliance

Employers must continue to meet their obligations after the foreign worker begins employment.

LOW-WAGE LMIA

Low-Wage Positions

Low-wage LMIA applications are subject to additional restrictions intended to limit reliance on temporary foreign workers.

01

Full-Time Employment

Low-wage positions under the program must generally be full-time, meaning at least 30 hours of work per week.

02

Low-Wage Cap

Limits may apply to the proportion of low-wage temporary foreign workers at a particular work location.

03

Regional Restrictions

Certain low-wage LMIA applications may be affected by unemployment rates or other regional restrictions.

04

Additional Employer Costs

Depending on the situation, employers may have additional obligations related to transportation, housing, insurance or worker protections.

GLOBAL TALENT STREAM

Hiring Specialized and Highly Skilled Talent

The Global Talent Stream is a specialized LMIA pathway designed for certain Canadian employers seeking highly skilled workers.

Category A may be available to qualifying employers referred by a designated referral partner for unique and specialized talent.

Category B may apply where the employer is hiring for an occupation appearing on the current Global Talent Occupations List.

Hiring Highly Skilled Talent?

If the occupation is highly specialized or appears on the Global Talent Occupations List, the Global Talent Stream may be worth reviewing.

GOVERNMENT FEE

The LMIA Processing Fee Is Generally $1,000 Per Position

In most LMIA streams, the employer must pay a government processing fee of $1,000 for each position requested.

For example, an LMIA application requesting two foreign worker positions would generally involve a $2,000 LMIA processing fee.

Certain agricultural positions and other specific circumstances may qualify for a fee exemption.

The Worker Cannot Pay the LMIA Fee

The employer cannot charge or recover the LMIA processing fee from the temporary foreign worker.

Review My LMIA Costs

RECRUITMENT FEES

Employers Must Protect Workers From Improper Recruitment Fees

Employers must ensure that temporary foreign workers are not charged or required to repay prohibited recruitment fees.

This obligation can extend to recruiters and other third parties acting on the employer's behalf.

Employers should carefully review recruitment arrangements and third-party practices before submitting an LMIA application.

Employer Responsibility Extends Beyond the Application

Using a recruiter does not remove the employer's responsibility to comply with Temporary Foreign Worker Program rules.

EMPLOYER ELIGIBILITY

The Employer Must Be Able to Support the Job Offer

Service Canada may review the employer's business and ability to meet the terms of the employment offer.

01

Legitimate Business

The employer must generally operate a legitimate business providing goods or services in Canada.

02

Genuine Job

The position must represent a genuine employment need within the business.

03

Ability to Pay

The employer may need to demonstrate the ability to meet the wage and employment commitments made in the LMIA application.

04

Compliance History

Previous compliance issues or violations may affect an employer's ability to participate in the Temporary Foreign Worker Program.

NOC CLASSIFICATION

Choosing the Correct NOC Code Is Important

The position must be classified under Canada's National Occupational Classification system.

The correct NOC should reflect the actual main duties and responsibilities of the position rather than simply the job title.

The NOC classification can affect wages, recruitment requirements, work permit eligibility and other parts of the immigration process.

Job Title Alone Is Not Enough

Two positions with similar titles can fall under different NOC codes depending on their actual duties.

TRANSITION PLAN

High-Wage Employers May Need a Transition Plan

A transition plan may be required for certain high-wage LMIA applications.

The plan describes actions the employer will take to recruit, retain or train Canadians and permanent residents or otherwise reduce reliance on temporary foreign workers.

When an employer submits future LMIA applications, Service Canada may review whether commitments made in an earlier transition plan were followed.

Transition Plans Create Future Commitments

Employers should propose realistic actions and maintain documentation showing that those commitments were implemented.

EMPLOYER COMPLIANCE

Employer Responsibilities Continue After Approval

Receiving a positive LMIA does not end the employer's responsibilities.

01

Pay the Required Wage

The worker should receive wages consistent with the terms approved through the LMIA process.

02

Provide the Agreed Job

The employer should provide employment that is substantially consistent with the approved occupation and working conditions.

03

Respect Employment Laws

Employers must comply with applicable federal, provincial and territorial employment standards.

04

Keep Records

Employers should maintain employment and compliance records as required under the program.

05

Cooperate With Inspections

Employers may be inspected to verify compliance with Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements.

06

Protect Workers

Employers must provide a workplace free from abuse and comply with worker-protection requirements.

NON-COMPLIANCE

Non-Compliance Can Have Serious Consequences

Employers participating in the Temporary Foreign Worker Program may be subject to inspections and compliance reviews.

Employers found non-compliant may face consequences such as warnings, monetary penalties, bans from hiring temporary foreign workers or publication of non-compliance information.

Employers should therefore view LMIA compliance as an ongoing responsibility, not simply an application requirement.

Compliance Starts Before Submission

Recruitment, job duties, wages and business records should be accurate from the beginning of the LMIA process.

PERMANENT RESIDENCE

LMIA Applications Can Also Be Connected to Permanent Residence

Certain employers may apply for an LMIA to support a skilled worker in connection with their permanent residence plans.

The requirements depend on the worker's occupation, immigration pathway and whether temporary work authorization is also being requested.

An LMIA used in connection with permanent residence should be planned together with the worker's broader immigration strategy.

LMIA EXEMPTIONS

Not Every Foreign Worker Requires an LMIA

Before beginning an LMIA application, employers should confirm whether the foreign worker may qualify under an LMIA-exempt work permit category.

Canada's International Mobility Program contains several LMIA-exempt work permit categories based on international agreements, significant benefit, intra-company transfers and other circumstances.

If a valid LMIA exemption exists, an employer may be able to hire the worker without completing the Temporary Foreign Worker Program LMIA process.

Check for an Exemption First

An LMIA is a detailed employer process. Before starting, confirm whether a simpler LMIA-exempt work permit pathway is available.

APPLICATION PROCESS

How the LMIA Process Works

A strong LMIA application begins before the application form is submitted.

1

Assess the Position

Review the occupation, NOC, wage, location and business need.

2

Identify the LMIA Stream

Determine whether the position falls under high-wage, low-wage, Global Talent or another specialized stream.

3

Complete Recruitment

Conduct the required recruitment and advertising for Canadians and permanent residents.

4

Prepare the LMIA Application

Compile business, recruitment, employment and stream-specific documentation.

5

Submit to Service Canada

Submit the application and pay the applicable LMIA processing fee.

6

Complete Employer Review

Service Canada may contact the employer to review the application and request additional information.

7

Receive the LMIA Decision

If the application is approved, the employer receives a positive LMIA decision.

8

Worker Applies for Work Permit

The employer provides the required documents to the foreign worker so they can proceed with the work permit application.

COMMON LMIA ISSUES

Common Problems Employers Should Avoid

LMIA applications are highly document-driven and inconsistencies can affect the outcome.

!

Incorrect NOC

The selected occupation does not accurately reflect the actual job duties.

!

Incorrect Wage

The employer uses a wage that does not meet the requirements for the occupation or LMIA stream.

!

Recruitment Errors

Advertisements do not meet the required content, timing or recruitment-method requirements.

!

Weak Business Need

The application does not clearly demonstrate why the position genuinely exists within the business.

!

Inconsistent Documents

The job posting, LMIA application, employment agreement and supporting documents contain different information.

!

Improper Worker Fees

LMIA or prohibited recruitment costs are charged back to the temporary foreign worker.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

LMIA Rules Can Change With Labour Market Conditions

Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements are regularly updated in response to labour market conditions and government policy.

Wage thresholds, low-wage restrictions, occupation lists and other eligibility requirements may change over time.

Employers should therefore confirm the current rules before starting recruitment or submitting an LMIA application.
