LABOUR MARKET IMPACT ASSESSMENT

What Is an LMIA?

A Labour Market Impact Assessment, commonly called an LMIA, is an assessment conducted by Employment and Social Development Canada and Service Canada.

It is used in many cases where a Canadian employer wants to hire a temporary foreign worker.

The assessment considers whether hiring the foreign worker is expected to have a positive or neutral effect on Canada's labour market.

A positive LMIA generally confirms that there is a need for a temporary foreign worker and that qualified Canadians or permanent residents are not available to fill the position.

WHO APPLIES?

The Employer Applies for the LMIA

The LMIA application is generally submitted by the Canadian employer, not by the foreign worker.

The employer must demonstrate that the position and business meet the applicable Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements.

If Service Canada issues a positive LMIA, the employer provides the required LMIA documents to the foreign worker.

The worker can then use those documents when applying for an employer-specific Canadian work permit.

Two Separate Processes

Step 1: Employer obtains the LMIA where required.

Step 2: Foreign worker applies for the appropriate work permit.

LMIA REQUIRED OR EXEMPT?

Not Every Foreign Worker Requires an LMIA

Employers should determine whether the position requires an LMIA before beginning the hiring process.

✓

LMIA-Required Position

Most employers hiring temporary foreign workers through the Temporary Foreign Worker Program need a positive LMIA before the worker applies for a work permit.

✓

LMIA-Exempt Position

Some workers qualify under an exemption, commonly through the International Mobility Program or another applicable exemption.

✓

Open Work Permit Holder

An employer generally does not need an LMIA simply to hire a person who already holds a valid open work permit.

!

Confirm Before Applying

Using the wrong employer process can delay or prevent the foreign worker from obtaining the required work authorization.

HIGH-WAGE & LOW-WAGE STREAMS

Which LMIA Stream Applies?

For many positions, the wage offered by the employer determines whether the LMIA is processed under the high-wage or low-wage stream.

H

High-Wage Positions

If the wage offered is at or above the applicable provincial or territorial hourly wage threshold, the employer generally applies under the high-wage stream.

L

Low-Wage Positions

If the offered wage is below the applicable provincial or territorial threshold, the employer generally applies under the low-wage stream.

WAGE REQUIREMENTS

The Wage Must Meet More Than One Test

The provincial or territorial hourly threshold determines whether the application falls into the high-wage or low-wage stream.

However, that threshold does not by itself determine whether the wage being offered is acceptable.

Employers must also meet the applicable prevailing-wage requirements for the occupation, location and circumstances.

Wage thresholds are updated periodically, so they should be checked when preparing each LMIA application rather than hard-coded into an immigration strategy.

Thresholds Change

The wage used to classify a position as high-wage or low-wage is updated by the government. Always use the current threshold.

EMPLOYER ASSESSMENT

What Does Service Canada Review?

An LMIA application involves an assessment of both the employer and the position being offered.

01

Genuine Business

The employer may need to demonstrate that it operates a genuine and legitimate business capable of providing the employment offered.

02

Genuine Job Offer

The position must represent genuine employment with duties, wages and working conditions consistent with program requirements.

03

Recruitment Efforts

Employers may need to demonstrate appropriate efforts to recruit qualified Canadians and permanent residents before turning to a temporary foreign worker.

04

Wage

The offered wage must meet the applicable wage and prevailing-wage requirements.

05

Working Conditions

Employment conditions must generally be consistent with Canadian employment standards and Temporary Foreign Worker Program requirements.

06

Labour Market Impact

Service Canada assesses the expected effect of hiring the foreign worker on Canada's labour market.

RECRUITMENT

Employers May Need to Recruit Canadians First

Recruitment is an important part of many LMIA applications.

Depending on the stream, an employer may need to advertise the position and demonstrate reasonable efforts to recruit or train Canadian citizens and permanent residents.

Recruitment requirements may address where the job is advertised, how long the advertisements must run and the information included in them.

Because the requirements vary by stream, employers should establish the correct recruitment strategy before beginning the advertising period.

Recruit Before Applying

In many LMIA streams, recruitment must be completed according to specific requirements before the employer submits the application.

LMIA DECISION

Positive and Negative LMIA Decisions

Submitting an LMIA application does not guarantee that Service Canada will approve the employer's request.

+

Positive LMIA

A positive LMIA indicates that hiring the temporary foreign worker is expected to have a positive or neutral labour market impact and that the employer may proceed with the next immigration steps.

−

Negative LMIA

A negative decision means the employer has not received the labour market approval needed to use that LMIA in support of the proposed employment.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A Positive LMIA Is Not a Work Permit

Receiving a positive LMIA does not by itself authorize a foreign worker to begin working in Canada.

The foreign national generally still needs to apply for and obtain the appropriate work authorization unless another legal basis already allows them to work.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada assesses the worker's work permit application separately from Service Canada's LMIA assessment.

LMIA ≠ Work Permit

The LMIA is the employer-side labour market assessment. The work permit is the worker's immigration authorization.

LMIA STREAMS

Different Positions May Use Different LMIA Streams

The Temporary Foreign Worker Program contains multiple streams with different requirements.

01

High-Wage Positions

Positions at or above the applicable provincial or territorial wage threshold generally use the high-wage stream.

02

Low-Wage Positions

Positions below the applicable wage threshold generally use the low-wage stream and may be subject to additional restrictions.

03

Agricultural Workers

Specific agricultural positions may be processed under dedicated agricultural or seasonal worker streams.

04

Global Talent

Certain highly skilled positions and qualifying employers may be eligible for specialized Temporary Foreign Worker Program processing.

05

Permanent Residence Support

Certain LMIA applications may be connected with an employer's effort to support a skilled worker's permanent residence pathway.

06

Other Specialized Streams

Additional requirements may apply to caregivers, academics and other types of employment depending on the circumstances.

APPLICATION PROCESS

A Typical LMIA Process

Exact requirements vary by stream and position, but the process generally involves several employer-side steps before a worker applies for a work permit.

1

Confirm LMIA Requirement

Determine whether the proposed employment needs an LMIA or qualifies for an exemption.

2

Identify the Correct Stream

Review the wage, occupation and employment circumstances to determine which LMIA program stream applies.

3

Complete Recruitment

Carry out the required recruitment and advertising activities where applicable.

4

Submit LMIA Application

The employer submits the required employer, position, recruitment and supporting documentation to Service Canada.

5

Worker Applies for Permit

After a positive LMIA, the foreign worker uses the applicable documents to apply for the required work permit.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

LMIA Requirements Change With the Position and Program Stream

An LMIA is not a single standardized application that works the same way for every employer.

Wage levels, occupation, work location, recruitment, business circumstances and the selected Temporary Foreign Worker Program stream can all affect the application requirements.

Government thresholds, caps and temporary program measures may also change over time.

Employers should therefore confirm the current requirements before advertising the position or submitting an LMIA application.
