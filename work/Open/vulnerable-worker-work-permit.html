Open Work Permit for Vulnerable Workers

Temporary foreign workers in Canada who are experiencing abuse, or are at risk of abuse in relation to their job, may be eligible for a temporary open work permit that allows them to leave the abusive employment situation.

VULNERABLE WORKERS

You Should Not Have to Stay in an Abusive Job to Keep Your Status

Many temporary foreign workers in Canada hold employer-specific work permits that authorize them to work only for the employer named on their permit.

This can create a particularly difficult situation when a worker experiences abuse, exploitation or threats connected to their employment.

Canada's Open Work Permit for Vulnerable Workers is designed to help eligible workers leave an abusive employment situation and look for a new employer without first having to obtain a new employer-specific work permit.

The permit is a temporary protective measure. Eligibility depends on the worker's current immigration status and evidence of abuse or risk of abuse in relation to the job.

PURPOSE OF THE PERMIT

Why Does This Work Permit Exist?

The vulnerable worker open work permit is intended to reduce the immigration-related pressure that can make it difficult for temporary foreign workers to leave abuse.

01

Leave an Abusive Employer

An eligible worker may be able to leave the employer named on their current work permit without first securing another employer-specific permit.

02

Work for a New Employer

Once approved, the open work permit generally allows the worker to seek employment with most eligible employers in Canada.

03

Reduce Employer Dependence

The permit can temporarily remove the worker's immigration status from being tied to the employer involved in the abusive situation.

04

Provide Time to Find a New Path

The permit is intended to provide time for the worker to leave the abusive situation, find new employment and pursue another appropriate work permit.

ELIGIBILITY

Who May Apply?

The program has specific immigration and workplace-related eligibility requirements.

01

You Are in Canada

You must be physically present in Canada when applying for this open work permit.

02

Abuse or Risk of Abuse

You must be experiencing abuse or be at risk of being abused in relation to your job in Canada.

03

Employer-Specific Work Permit

You generally must currently hold a valid employer-specific work permit that names your employer.

04

Extension Already Submitted

You may also qualify if you applied to extend your employer-specific work permit before it expired and IRCC has not yet made a decision.

EMPLOYER-SPECIFIC WORK PERMIT

This Program Is Designed for Workers Tied to an Employer

An employer-specific work permit, sometimes informally called a closed work permit, generally lists the employer for whom the foreign worker is authorized to work.

A worker cannot normally leave that employer and immediately begin working for another employer without obtaining new work authorization.

The vulnerable worker open work permit creates a temporary exception for eligible workers facing abuse or the risk of abuse connected to their employment.

Open Work Permit Holders Are Different

This specific program is generally intended for workers whose authorization is tied to an employer, rather than workers who already hold an unrestricted open work permit.

WHAT COUNTS AS ABUSE?

Abuse Is Not Limited to Physical Violence

Workplace abuse can take many forms. IRCC recognizes physical, sexual, financial, psychological abuse and reprisal.

01

Physical Abuse

Physical harm, threats of violence or being forced to work in unsafe conditions may be relevant evidence of abuse.

02

Sexual Abuse or Harassment

Unwanted sexual touching, sexual comments or other sexually abusive conduct may fall within the definition of abuse.

03

Financial Abuse

Taking wages, withholding money, improper recruitment fees or other financial exploitation may be relevant.

04

Psychological Abuse

Threats, intimidation, insults, isolation, coercion or controlling behaviour may constitute psychological abuse.

05

Unsafe Living Conditions

Unsafe or unsanitary employer-provided housing may also be relevant where it forms part of the abusive employment situation.

06

Reprisal

Demotion, dismissal, discipline or threats of punishment because a worker reported non-compliance or cooperated with an inspection may constitute reprisal.

POSSIBLE WARNING SIGNS

Examples of Situations That May Need Review

Every case depends on its facts, but these are examples of conduct that may be relevant to a vulnerable worker application.

!

Unpaid or Withheld Wages

Your employer keeps part of your pay, does not pay agreed wages or demands that you return part of your wages.

!

Threats About Immigration Status

Someone threatens you with dismissal, deportation or immigration consequences to control your behaviour.

!

Unsafe Work

You are forced to perform dangerous work or work under conditions that put your health or safety at risk.

!

Isolation or Control

Your employer or another person connected to the employment controls where you go or prevents you from communicating with others.

!

Recruitment Fraud

A recruiter or third party charges improper fees, makes false promises about the job or otherwise exploits the worker.

!

Retaliation for Complaining

You are threatened, disciplined, demoted or dismissed because you raised concerns about working conditions.

RISK OF ABUSE

You Do Not Necessarily Have to Wait Until Abuse Becomes Worse

The program applies not only to workers who are already experiencing abuse but also to workers who are at risk of being abused in relation to their job.

This means IRCC may consider circumstances showing a genuine risk even where the worker has not experienced every possible form of abuse.

The application should clearly explain the worker's circumstances and why they believe abuse is occurring or is likely to occur.

You Do Not Need to Endure More Harm First

The purpose of this permit is protective. Evidence of a genuine risk of abuse can be relevant even before the situation escalates further.

SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

How Can You Show Abuse or Risk of Abuse?

The application should provide enough information and evidence for an immigration officer to understand what is happening.

01

Your Own Explanation

A detailed written explanation can describe the events, working conditions, threats, financial issues and other circumstances relevant to the application.

02

Messages and Communications

Emails, text messages, messaging-app conversations, voicemails or other communications may support the worker's account.

03

Employment Records

Employment agreements, schedules, pay records, pay stubs and bank records may help demonstrate discrepancies or abusive employment practices.

04

Photos or Other Records

Photographs or other records may help document unsafe work, housing conditions, injuries or other relevant circumstances.

05

Third-Party Information

Information from co-workers, community organizations, health professionals, police or other third parties may be relevant when available.

06

Immigration and Job Documents

Your work permit, employment documents and information about the employer help establish the immigration and employment context of the case.

EVIDENCE

Evidence Can Take Different Forms

Workplace abuse does not always happen in a way that creates one obvious official document.

Applicants should provide the evidence reasonably available to them and clearly explain what occurred, who was involved and how the situation relates to their employment in Canada.

The overall circumstances should be presented clearly and consistently rather than relying on one single type of evidence.

Build the Full Picture

Messages, wage records, workplace documents, photographs and a detailed personal explanation can help an officer understand the circumstances of the case.

WHERE TO APPLY

The Application Must Be Made From Inside Canada

The vulnerable worker open work permit is specifically designed for eligible temporary foreign workers who are currently in Canada.

The application is generally submitted online through the applicable IRCC process.

This work permit cannot be applied for at a Canadian port of entry.

Do Not Try to Apply at the Border

IRCC specifically requires eligible vulnerable workers to apply through the designated application process rather than at a port of entry.

GOVERNMENT FEES

There Are No Work Permit Application Fees for Eligible Vulnerable Workers

Vulnerable worker open work permit applicants are exempt from both the standard work permit processing fee and the open work permit holder fee.

IRCC has a specific online application process that allows eligible applicants to indicate the applicable fee exemption.

Biometrics are also handled under special procedures for this application category.

Work Permit Fee: $0

Eligible vulnerable workers are exempt from the work permit processing fee and the open work permit holder fee.

BIOMETRICS

Special Biometrics Instructions Apply

Vulnerable worker applicants are not asked to provide biometrics at the initial application stage in the usual way.

If an immigration officer determines that biometrics are required, IRCC can request them after the application has been submitted.

Applicants should follow the specific vulnerable worker application instructions rather than assuming the standard work permit biometrics process applies.

Follow the Special Application Instructions

Vulnerable worker applications have specific fee and biometrics procedures within the online application system.

FAMILY MEMBERS

Can Your Family Members Receive Open Work Permits?

Family members who accompanied the vulnerable worker to Canada may also be eligible for an open work permit if the vulnerable worker's application is approved.

Each eligible family member must submit their own work permit application.

Their applications may be submitted together with the vulnerable worker's application.

Family Applications Are Separate

Approval of the principal worker's permit does not automatically issue work permits to family members. Separate applications are required.

TEMPORARY SOLUTION

A Vulnerable Worker Open Work Permit Cannot Be Renewed

This permit is intended as a temporary solution that gives an eligible worker time to leave an abusive situation and find another employer.

The work permit has an expiry date and cannot be renewed as another vulnerable worker open work permit.

If the worker wants to remain and continue working in Canada, another appropriate work permit should be obtained before the vulnerable worker permit expires.

Plan the Next Work Permit Early

Once you are safe and working under the open permit, the next step is usually to identify a longer-term work authorization strategy before the permit expires.

Plan My Next Work Permit

AFTER APPROVAL

Finding a New Employer

Once the vulnerable worker open work permit is approved, the worker generally has the flexibility to work for most employers in Canada.

The worker can use this period to leave the abusive employer, obtain new employment and determine what work permit will be appropriate after the open permit expires.

Depending on the new job and the worker's immigration circumstances, the next work permit may involve an LMIA or an available LMIA-exempt category.

The Open Permit Is a Bridge to Safer Employment

The goal is not only to leave the abusive employer, but also to establish a sustainable immigration and employment plan afterward.

EMPLOYER COMPLIANCE

What Happens to the Employer?

If IRCC approves a vulnerable worker open work permit, the government may later inspect the employer to determine whether temporary foreign worker program requirements were followed.

IRCC states that it may contact the employer for the purpose of conducting an inspection.

The worker may also be asked to provide additional information for an employer inspection, but providing that additional inspection information is voluntary.

Your Work Permit Application Is About Your Protection

The vulnerable worker permit exists to help the worker leave an abusive situation. Employer compliance action is a separate government process.

WORK AUTHORIZATION

Can You Immediately Start Working for Another Employer?

Applying for a vulnerable worker open work permit does not automatically mean that the worker can immediately begin working for a different employer.

Until new work authorization applies, the worker must respect the conditions of their existing immigration documents.

The worker's current status and work permit conditions should be reviewed carefully before starting employment with another employer.

Do Not Start Unauthorized Work

Leaving an abusive employment situation and having authorization to work for a new employer are separate questions. Confirm your work authorization first.

APPLICATION PROCESS

How the Vulnerable Worker Application Works

The application should focus on both immigration eligibility and the facts surrounding the abusive employment situation.

1

Confirm Immigration Eligibility

Review your location in Canada, employer-specific work permit and current temporary resident status.

2

Document the Situation

Prepare a clear chronology explaining the abuse or risk of abuse connected to your employment.

3

Gather Supporting Evidence

Collect messages, employment records, financial records, photographs and other relevant documentation that is reasonably available.

4

Submit the Online Application

Apply through the specific IRCC process for an Open Work Permit for Vulnerable Workers.

5

Respond to IRCC if Required

IRCC may request additional information or ask the applicant to participate in an interview before making a decision.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

Important Things to Understand

This is a special protective immigration measure and should not be treated like an ordinary open work permit category.

!

Abuse Is Not Only Physical

Financial, psychological, sexual abuse and reprisal can also be relevant under the program.

!

Being Unhappy at Work Is Not the Same Thing

A difficult workplace or disagreement with an employer does not automatically establish abuse. The specific facts must be reviewed.

!

It Is Not a Permanent Work Permit

The permit is temporary and cannot simply be renewed under the same vulnerable worker category.

!

No New Job Offer Is Required First

The worker does not need to remain in the abusive job until another employer obtains an LMIA or other employer-specific authorization.

!

You Must Be in Canada

This specific protective work permit cannot be used as an overseas work permit pathway.

!

Approval Does Not Decide the Employer's Liability

The work permit protects the worker. Employer inspections and other enforcement actions are separate processes.

PERSONAL SAFETY

Immigration Status Should Not Prevent You From Seeking Help

If you are experiencing workplace abuse, your personal safety may require attention in addition to your immigration status.

Depending on the circumstances, support may also be available through police, employment standards authorities, worker support organizations, settlement agencies or other community services.

Immigration advice can address the work permit and status issues, but it does not replace emergency, legal, medical or social-service assistance when those services are needed.

Immediate Danger?

If you are in immediate danger in Canada, contact emergency services by calling 9-1-1.
