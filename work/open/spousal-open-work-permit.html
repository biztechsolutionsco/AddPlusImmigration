SPOUSAL OPEN WORK PERMIT

Can Your Spouse Work in Canada?

A Spousal Open Work Permit may allow an eligible spouse or common-law partner to work for most employers in Canada without first obtaining a specific job offer.

However, being married to or in a common-law relationship with a student or worker in Canada does not automatically make someone eligible for an open work permit.

Eligibility depends on the immigration situation of the person in Canada. Different requirements apply to spouses of international students, spouses of foreign workers and spouses or partners who are being sponsored for permanent residence.

Because these rules have changed significantly in recent years, it is important to identify the correct work permit category before applying.

OPEN WORK PERMIT

What Does a Spousal Open Work Permit Allow?

Unlike an employer-specific work permit, an open work permit is generally not tied to one specific employer.

01

No Specific Job Offer Required

An eligible applicant generally does not need to obtain a Canadian job offer before applying for an open work permit.

02

Work for Most Employers

An open work permit generally allows the holder to work for most eligible employers anywhere in Canada.

03

Change Employers

Because the permit is generally not tied to one employer, the holder may usually change jobs without applying for a new employer-specific work permit.

04

Work Authorization Is Temporary

The work permit remains a temporary immigration document and does not itself provide permanent resident status.

05

Conditions May Apply

Medical examination requirements or other work permit conditions may affect the types of employment an applicant is authorized to perform.

06

Eligibility Must Be Established

Open work permits are available only in specific circumstances. An applicant cannot simply choose an open work permit because their spouse is in Canada.

WHO MAY QUALIFY

Common Spousal Open Work Permit Categories

The eligibility requirements depend on why your spouse or common-law partner is currently authorized to study, work or remain in Canada.

01

Spouses of International Students

Spouses and common-law partners of certain international students may qualify if the student is enrolled in an eligible program of study.

02

Spouses of Foreign Workers

Spouses and common-law partners of certain foreign workers may qualify depending on the worker's occupation, work authorization and immigration pathway.

03

Sponsored Spouses and Partners

A spouse or partner who is in Canada and being sponsored for permanent residence may be eligible for an open work permit while the permanent residence application is being processed.

INTERNATIONAL STUDENTS

Spouses of International Students

Spouses and common-law partners of international students are no longer automatically eligible for an open work permit simply because the student holds a Canadian study permit.

Under the current rules, the international student generally must hold a valid study permit and be enrolled in a qualifying program.

This includes certain master's degree programs, doctoral degree programs, designated professional degree programs and certain specifically identified eligible programs.

The Student's Program Matters

The type and length of the principal student's program can determine whether their spouse or common-law partner is eligible for an open work permit.

FOREIGN WORKERS

Spouses of Foreign Workers

The spouse or common-law partner of a foreign worker may be eligible for an open work permit, but the rules depend on the principal worker's occupation and immigration situation.

For many foreign workers who are not already on an eligible permanent residence pathway, the principal worker must be employed or expected to work in a qualifying occupation.

The remaining validity of the principal worker's work authorization can also be important.

HIGH-SKILLED WORKERS

Occupation Requirements for Many Foreign Workers

For foreign workers who are not relying on a separate eligible permanent residence pathway, current rules generally focus on the worker's occupation and remaining work authorization.

01

TEER 0

Workers employed or expected to be employed in management occupations classified as TEER 0 may support an eligible spouse's open work permit application.

02

TEER 1

Workers in professional occupations classified as TEER 1 may also support an eligible spouse or common-law partner.

03

Selected TEER 2 and TEER 3

Only certain occupations within TEER 2 and TEER 3 qualify under the current family open work permit rules.

04

Work Permit Validity

In many high-skilled worker cases, the principal worker's work authorization must remain valid for at least 16 months after IRCC receives the spouse's application.

Some workers may qualify under separate family open work permit measures with different requirements. For example, certain workers under designated Significant Investment Projects in British Columbia and certain foreign-trained health professionals recruited by Quebec may be exempt from the usual 16-month work authorization requirement.

PERMANENT RESIDENCE PATHWAYS

Different Rules Apply to Workers on Eligible PR Pathways

Some foreign workers who have already applied for permanent residence through an eligible economic immigration program, or who are working under certain designated pathways to permanent residence, may support family open work permit applications under separate rules.

In some of these cases, eligibility is not limited to TEER 0, 1 or selected TEER 2 and 3 occupations. Certain workers in TEER 4 or 5 occupations may also qualify where they meet the requirements of an eligible permanent residence pathway.

The required remaining validity of the principal worker's work authorization may also be different. For certain workers who have already applied for permanent residence, the work permit generally must remain valid for at least six months after IRCC receives the family member's application.

PR Pathway Cases Require Separate Review

Do not assume that the standard TEER rule automatically determines eligibility if the principal worker has already applied for permanent residence or is working under an eligible PR pathway.

SPOUSAL SPONSORSHIP

Open Work Permits During Spousal Sponsorship

A spouse, common-law partner or conjugal partner living in Canada and being sponsored for permanent residence may be eligible for an open work permit while the permanent residence application is processed.

01

Living in Canada

The sponsored spouse or partner must be living in Canada with their sponsor.

02

Genuine Relationship

The applicant must be in a genuine qualifying relationship with the person sponsoring them.

03

Permanent Residence Application

The applicant must be included in a qualifying spouse or partner permanent residence application.

04

Acknowledgement of Receipt

In most cases, the applicant applies after IRCC confirms that the permanent residence application is complete and issues an acknowledgement of receipt.

05

Temporary Resident Status

The applicant generally must have valid temporary resident status, maintained status or be eligible for restoration.

06

Limited AOR Exception

An applicant may be able to apply without an acknowledgement of receipt if their work permit, study permit or temporary resident status will expire in two weeks or less and the applicable permanent residence application has already been submitted.

Spouse or Common-Law Partner

The applicant must establish a qualifying relationship with the person whose immigration status makes the open work permit possible.

✓

✓

✓

PERMIT VALIDITY

How Long Will the Spousal Open Work Permit Be Valid?

The length of a spousal open work permit depends on the immigration category and the validity of the documents supporting the application.

A spousal open work permit is normally issued for a period that corresponds with the validity of the principal spouse or common-law partner's study or work permit.

The principal applicant's permit validity can therefore affect both eligibility and the length of the spouse's open work permit.

Passport validity and other immigration requirements can also limit the period for which a work permit is issued.

Check Expiry Dates Before Applying

The principal applicant's immigration document and the spouse's passport should be reviewed before submission to identify potential validity issues.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

A Spouse in Canada Does Not Automatically Mean an Open Work Permit

Current eligibility rules are more restrictive than they were in the past.

!

Not Every International Student Qualifies

Many college, undergraduate and shorter study programs do not make a spouse eligible for a new spousal open work permit.

!

Not Every Foreign Worker Qualifies

The principal worker's occupation, work authorization and permanent residence pathway may all affect eligibility.

!

TEER 2 or 3 Does Not Automatically Qualify

For many foreign worker cases, only selected TEER 2 and TEER 3 occupations are eligible under the current rules.

!

Marriage Alone Is Not Enough

A valid relationship is required, but the principal applicant must also meet the applicable immigration criteria.

WORK AUTHORIZATION

Can You Work While the Application Is Being Processed?

Submitting a Spousal Open Work Permit application does not automatically give every applicant permission to begin working in Canada.

Whether an applicant can continue working while waiting for a decision depends on their existing immigration status, current work authorization and whether they meet the requirements for maintained status as a worker.

Someone who is currently only authorized to visit or study in Canada should not assume that submitting an open work permit application permits them to start working.

Do Not Work Without Authorization

Working without authorization can create serious immigration consequences. Confirm your ability to work before starting employment.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Spousal Open Work Permit Rules Have Changed

Canada has introduced significant changes to family open work permit eligibility, particularly for spouses of international students and temporary foreign workers.

Information that applied to an application several years ago may no longer reflect current eligibility requirements.

Before applying, review the principal applicant's current study or work permit, program of study, occupation, NOC code, work permit expiry date and any permanent residence application or pathway.
