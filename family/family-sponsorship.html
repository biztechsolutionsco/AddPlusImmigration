FAMILY SPONSORSHIP

Can You Sponsor Another Family Member?

Canada's Family Class immigration system allows eligible sponsors to help certain family members become permanent residents.

Some relationships have their own sponsorship categories, including spouses and partners, dependent children, and parents and grandparents.

Sponsorship of other relatives is much more limited and is only available when specific conditions are met.

Before applying, it is important to confirm both that the sponsor is eligible and that the family relationship falls within a category that can actually be sponsored.

ELIGIBLE RELATIVES

Which Relatives May Be Sponsored?

Sponsorship of relatives outside the main spouse, child and parent categories is only available in specific circumstances.

01

Orphaned Brother or Sister

An orphaned brother or sister may potentially be sponsored if they are related by blood or adoption, under 18, both parents have died, and they are not married or common-law.

02

Orphaned Niece or Nephew

An orphaned niece or nephew may potentially qualify under the same narrow requirements relating to age, parental death and marital status.

03

Orphaned Grandchild

An orphaned grandchild under 18 may potentially be sponsored if both parents have died and the other applicable requirements are met.

04

One Other Relative

In very limited circumstances, a person may be able to sponsor one relative of any age who is related by blood or adoption.

05

Accompanying Family Members

Where an eligible relative has a spouse, partner or dependent children who will accompany them, those family members may need to be included in the same application.

06

Separate Sponsorship Categories

Spouses, partners, dependent children, parents and grandparents are generally addressed through their own specific sponsorship categories.

ORPHANED RELATIVES

Strict Requirements Apply

Sponsorship of an orphaned sibling, niece, nephew or grandchild is limited to situations where all of the required conditions are met.

The relative must generally be under 18, related to the sponsor by blood or adoption, have lost both parents, and be unmarried and not in a common-law relationship.

A child is not considered eligible under this rule simply because a parent is missing, incarcerated, unable to care for them or has abandoned them.

Both Parents Must Have Died

IRCC's orphaned-relative category is narrow. Having one deceased parent, an absent parent or a parent who cannot provide care does not by itself satisfy the orphan requirement.

ONE OTHER RELATIVE

The "Other Relative" Rule Is Very Limited

In exceptional circumstances, a sponsor may be able to sponsor one relative of any age who is related to them by blood or adoption.

To qualify, the sponsor generally must not have a living close relative whom they could sponsor instead, such as a spouse, partner, child, parent, grandparent or certain orphaned relatives.

The sponsor also generally cannot have another relative in Canada who is already a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or registered Indian under the Indian Act.

Because the conditions are restrictive, simply having an aunt, uncle, cousin, sibling or other relative abroad does not normally mean that person can be sponsored.

Sometimes Called the "Lonely Canadian" Rule

This is an informal description sometimes used for the limited provision allowing sponsorship of one other relative. Eligibility depends on the sponsor's complete family situation.

SPONSOR ELIGIBILITY

General Requirements for the Sponsor

Sponsorship creates significant legal and financial responsibilities for the person bringing a relative to Canada.

01

At Least 18

The sponsor must be at least 18 years old.

02

Eligible Canadian Status

The sponsor must generally be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or a person registered in Canada under the Indian Act.

03

Live in Canada

For sponsorship of other relatives, the sponsor's primary residence generally must be in Canada from application through decision.

04

Meet Income Requirements

The sponsor must meet the applicable income guidelines for the sponsorship application.

05

Sign an Undertaking

The sponsor commits to financially support the sponsored relative and applicable accompanying family members.

06

No Sponsorship Bar

Bankruptcy, certain criminal convictions, social assistance, unpaid support obligations or previous sponsorship defaults can affect eligibility.

FINANCIAL RESPONSIBILITY

Sponsorship Is a Long-Term Commitment

A sponsor must sign an undertaking agreeing to financially support the sponsored relative and applicable accompanying family members.

Depending on the sponsored person's age and relationship to the sponsor, the undertaking period can last for many years and may extend up to 20 years.

If a sponsored relative receives certain social assistance during the undertaking period, the sponsor may be responsible for repaying the government.

Up to 20 Years

The sponsorship obligation can continue for a substantial period after the relative becomes a permanent resident. The exact undertaking depends on the relationship and circumstances.

SPONSORED RELATIVE

The Relative Must Also Qualify to Come to Canada

Sponsor eligibility alone does not guarantee permanent residence for the family member.

✓

Qualifying Relationship

The family relationship must meet the requirements of the sponsorship category.

✓

Required Documentation

Civil-status and other records may be required to establish the relationship between sponsor and applicant.

✓

Medical Examination

The applicant and applicable family members may need to complete an immigration medical examination.

✓

Admissibility

The person being sponsored must meet Canada's applicable immigration admissibility requirements.

APPLICATION PROCESS

How Family Sponsorship Works

Applications require both sponsor eligibility and permanent residence eligibility to be established.

1

Identify the Relationship

Determine whether the relative falls within a category that can be sponsored.

2

Confirm Sponsor Eligibility

Review Canadian status, residence, income and any possible sponsorship restrictions.

3

Prepare Family Evidence

Gather civil-status records and other documentation supporting the family relationship.

4

Submit the Application

Complete the sponsorship and permanent residence application according to IRCC's current submission process.

5

Complete IRCC Processing

Respond to any biometrics, medical, background or additional-document requests during processing.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Being Related Does Not Automatically Mean You Can Sponsor Someone

Canada's family sponsorship program does not allow a Canadian citizen or permanent resident to sponsor any relative simply because they are related.

The relationship must fall within a recognized sponsorship category and all applicable requirements must be met.

Cases involving siblings, nieces, nephews, grandchildren, cousins, aunts or uncles should therefore be assessed carefully before an application is prepared.
