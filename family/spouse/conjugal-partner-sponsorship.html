CONJUGAL PARTNER SPONSORSHIP

What Is a Conjugal Partner?

The conjugal partner category is intended for certain couples who are in a genuine, committed and mutually interdependent relationship but are neither married nor able to qualify as common-law partners.

A conjugal partner must generally have been in an exclusive relationship with the sponsor for at least one year.

The relationship should demonstrate significant emotional, physical, financial and social interdependence similar to a marriage or common-law partnership.

Importantly, this category is not simply an alternative for couples who prefer not to marry or who have not yet lived together for 12 months. There must generally be meaningful barriers preventing marriage or cohabitation.

ELIGIBILITY

Key Conjugal Partner Requirements

Conjugal partner sponsorship is a specific category and requires more than simply showing that two people are in a romantic relationship.

01

At Least 18 Years Old

The sponsored conjugal partner must generally be at least 18 years of age.

02

At Least One Year

The couple must generally have been in an exclusive and mutually interdependent relationship for at least one year.

03

Genuine Relationship

The relationship must be genuine and not entered into primarily for the purpose of obtaining permanent residence.

04

Not Married

The couple cannot already qualify under the spouse category as legally married spouses.

05

Not Common-Law

The couple cannot already qualify as common-law partners based on at least 12 consecutive months of qualifying cohabitation.

06

Partner Outside Canada

The person being sponsored in the conjugal partner category must live outside Canada.

A CRITICAL REQUIREMENT

Why Can't You Marry or Live Together?

This is one of the most important questions in a conjugal partner sponsorship case.

The category is designed for couples who would ordinarily be expected to marry or establish common-law status but cannot reasonably do so because of significant barriers beyond the normal difficulties of a long-distance relationship.

IRCC may consider legal, immigration, social, cultural, religious or other substantial circumstances that prevent marriage or cohabitation.

The specific barrier should be clearly explained and supported by appropriate evidence.

Not Simply a Long-Distance Category

Living in different countries does not by itself establish eligibility as conjugal partners. The reason the couple cannot marry or establish common-law cohabitation is a central part of the case.

POSSIBLE BARRIERS

Circumstances That May Be Relevant

Every case is assessed individually. The following are examples of circumstances that may be relevant when a couple cannot marry or establish common-law status.

01

Legal Barriers

The laws of a country may prevent the couple from legally marrying or otherwise recognizing their relationship.

02

Immigration Barriers

Immigration or visa restrictions may prevent the couple from living together long enough to establish common-law status.

03

Marital Status Barriers

A partner may remain legally married to another person in a jurisdiction where obtaining a divorce is not possible.

04

Same-Sex Relationship Restrictions

Laws or conditions affecting same-sex relationships or same-sex marriage may prevent the couple from marrying or living safely together.

05

Religious or Cultural Barriers

Serious religious, cultural or social circumstances may be relevant where they genuinely prevent the couple from marrying or living together.

06

Risk of Persecution

A relationship may expose one or both partners to serious legal or social consequences in the country where they live.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Situations That Do Not Automatically Make a Couple Conjugal Partners

The conjugal partner category should not be treated as a substitute for spouse or common-law sponsorship.

!

Long-Distance Dating

Maintaining a long-distance relationship does not by itself establish conjugal partner eligibility.

!

Choosing Not to Marry

A personal preference not to marry, without a significant barrier preventing marriage or cohabitation, is generally different from the circumstances the conjugal category is intended to address.

!

Not Yet Living Together for 12 Months

A couple does not automatically become eligible as conjugal partners simply because they have not yet completed the 12-month common-law period.

!

Ordinary Travel Difficulties

The circumstances preventing marriage or cohabitation should be examined carefully rather than assuming ordinary inconvenience is sufficient.

RELATIONSHIP EVIDENCE

Demonstrating a Mutually Interdependent Relationship

IRCC considers whether the relationship reflects a genuine commitment and interdependence similar to a marriage or common-law partnership.

01

Emotional Connection

Evidence may demonstrate a committed, ongoing emotional relationship and the couple's involvement in each other's lives.

02

Time Together

Visits, travel history and other evidence can help show how the couple has maintained and developed the relationship.

03

Financial Connection

Financial support, shared expenses, beneficiary arrangements or other financial connections may demonstrate interdependence.

04

Communication

Communication records can help demonstrate the continuity and development of a relationship maintained across distance.

05

Social Recognition

Where available, evidence showing that family members, friends or others recognize the couple's relationship may be relevant.

06

Future Plans

Evidence of plans for a shared future can help demonstrate the couple's commitment to establishing a life together.

SUPPORTING DOCUMENTATION

Evidence Should Address Both the Relationship and the Barrier

A conjugal partner application generally needs to explain two connected issues: the genuine nature of the relationship and why the couple has been unable to marry or establish common-law status.

Depending on the circumstances, evidence may include immigration records, visa refusals, legal documents, country information, travel history, correspondence, proof of attempted visits or other documentation relevant to the particular barrier.

The appropriate evidence will vary significantly from one case to another.

Barrier Evidence Matters

A strong relationship alone does not necessarily establish conjugal partner eligibility. The circumstances preventing marriage or cohabitation should also be clearly documented.

SPONSOR ELIGIBILITY

Who Can Sponsor a Conjugal Partner?

The sponsor must meet Canada's general spouse and partner sponsorship requirements.

01

At Least 18 Years Old

The sponsor must generally be at least 18 years old.

02

Eligible Canadian Status

The sponsor must generally be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or a person registered in Canada under the Indian Act.

03

Residence Requirements

Permanent residents generally must live in Canada. Canadian citizens living outside Canada may be able to sponsor if they can demonstrate plans to live in Canada when the sponsored person becomes a permanent resident.

04

Sponsorship Undertaking

The sponsor must accept the applicable financial responsibilities toward the sponsored partner.

05

Sponsorship Restrictions

Previous sponsorships, certain financial defaults, criminal matters and other circumstances can affect eligibility to sponsor.

06

Genuine Sponsorship

Both the sponsor and applicant must present a genuine qualifying relationship that meets Canada's immigration requirements.

APPLICATION PROCESS

How Conjugal Partner Sponsorship Works

The application requires careful assessment of both the relationship and the circumstances preventing marriage or common-law cohabitation.

1

Confirm the Category

Determine whether the relationship truly fits the conjugal partner category rather than spouse or common-law sponsorship.

2

Assess the Barriers

Identify and document the circumstances preventing marriage or qualifying cohabitation.

3

Prepare Relationship Evidence

Gather documentation showing the genuine, committed and mutually interdependent nature of the relationship.

4

Submit Both Applications

The principal applicant submits the sponsorship application and permanent residence application together online.

5

Complete IRCC Processing

Complete applicable biometrics, medical examinations, background checks and any additional requests made during processing.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Conjugal Partner Sponsorship Is a Narrow Category

The conjugal partner category exists to address genuine relationships where circumstances beyond the couple's reasonable control have prevented marriage or qualifying cohabitation.

It should not automatically be selected simply because a couple lives in different countries or has not yet accumulated 12 months of cohabitation.

Before applying, the relationship history, efforts to spend time together, ability to marry or cohabit and the specific barriers involved should all be reviewed carefully.
