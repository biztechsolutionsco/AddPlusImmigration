SAME-SEX SPONSORSHIP

Same-Sex Couples Have Access to Canada's Family Sponsorship Program

Canadian immigration rules allow eligible Canadian citizens and permanent residents to sponsor a same-sex spouse or partner for permanent residence.

There is no separate immigration category requiring same-sex couples to meet a different standard. The appropriate sponsorship pathway depends on the legal and factual nature of your relationship.

A same-sex couple may qualify as spouses, common-law partners or, in certain circumstances, conjugal partners.

The correct category is important because each relationship type has its own legal requirements and supporting evidence.

At a Glance

Relationship Types Spouse, common-law partner or conjugal partner
Minimum Age Generally at least 18 years old
Main Requirement A genuine qualifying relationship
Income Requirement Generally no minimum income requirement
Final Goal Canadian Permanent Residence

RELATIONSHIP CATEGORIES

Which Sponsorship Category Applies?

Same-sex couples may qualify under one of three partner sponsorship categories depending on their circumstances.

01

Spouse

You may qualify as spouses if you are legally married and the marriage is recognized for Canadian immigration purposes.

02

Common-Law Partner

A couple may qualify as common-law partners after living together in a conjugal relationship for at least 12 consecutive months, subject to the applicable rules.

03

Conjugal Partner

This category may apply in limited situations where a couple has been in a genuine interdependent relationship for at least one year but significant barriers prevent them from marrying or living together.

MARRIAGES OUTSIDE CANADA

Is a Same-Sex Marriage From Another Country Recognized?

A marriage performed outside Canada generally needs to be legally recognized both under the law of the country where the marriage took place and under Canadian law.

This can create additional questions for couples who live in or have connections to countries where same-sex marriage is not legally recognized.

If a marriage does not meet the requirements for recognition, another relationship category, such as common-law or conjugal partner sponsorship, may need to be considered depending on the couple's circumstances.

Married Outside Canada?

The place where the marriage occurred and the applicable marriage laws can be important. The relationship should be reviewed before selecting the sponsorship category.

UNIQUE CIRCUMSTANCES

When Legal or Social Barriers Affect the Relationship

Some same-sex couples cannot safely marry, live together or openly maintain their relationship because of conditions in another country.

01

Same-Sex Marriage Is Illegal

Some couples may live in jurisdictions where they cannot legally marry because same-sex marriage is not recognized.

02

Same-Sex Relationships Are Restricted

In some countries, laws or government practices may restrict or criminalize same-sex relationships.

03

Safety or Persecution Concerns

Couples may face serious social, cultural or safety barriers that affect their ability to live openly together.

04

Immigration Barriers

Visa or immigration restrictions may prevent a couple from establishing a shared residence in the same country.

05

Family or Cultural Circumstances

Serious social or cultural barriers may form part of the overall circumstances that explain why a couple cannot marry or live together.

06

Conjugal Partner Considerations

Where substantial barriers prevent marriage or cohabitation, the conjugal partner category may be relevant if all applicable requirements are met.

SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

Showing That Your Relationship Is Genuine

The evidence available to each couple can be very different, particularly where privacy or safety concerns have affected the relationship.

✓

Relationship History

Evidence can show how you met, how the relationship developed and the important stages of your life together.

✓

Communication

Messages, calls and other communication may help demonstrate an ongoing relationship, especially during periods spent apart.

✓

Visits and Time Together

Travel records, photographs and other documents may help establish the time you have spent together.

✓

Shared Life

Evidence of shared responsibilities, residence, finances or future plans may help demonstrate the nature of the relationship.

✓

Family and Friends

Evidence showing recognition of the relationship by people who know the couple can also be relevant.

✓

Evidence of Barriers

For cases involving barriers to marriage or cohabitation, evidence explaining those barriers may be particularly important.

EVIDENCE CHALLENGES

What If You Had to Keep Your Relationship Private?

Some same-sex couples may not have the same types of public relationship evidence that other couples commonly provide.

For example, family members may not know about the relationship, photographs may be limited, joint accounts may not be practical, or the couple may have avoided documenting their relationship because of safety concerns.

A lack of one particular type of evidence does not automatically determine the outcome of an application. The complete circumstances of the relationship should be explained clearly and supported by the evidence that is reasonably available.

Limited Traditional Evidence?

The purpose of the application is not simply to collect as many documents as possible. The evidence should help present a clear, credible and consistent history of the relationship.

SPONSOR ELIGIBILITY

General Sponsor Requirements

The sponsor must meet the same general family sponsorship requirements that apply to other spouse and partner sponsorship cases.

01

At Least 18

The sponsor must generally be at least 18 years old.

02

Eligible Canadian Status

A sponsor must generally be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or a person registered in Canada under the Indian Act.

03

Residence Requirements

Permanent residents generally must live in Canada. Different rules can apply to Canadian citizens who are living abroad and planning to return to Canada.

04

Sponsorship Undertaking

The sponsor must accept financial responsibilities toward the person being sponsored.

05

Sponsor Must Be Eligible

Previous sponsorships, financial defaults, certain criminal matters and other circumstances can affect eligibility to sponsor.

06

Genuine Relationship

The relationship must be genuine and not entered into primarily for the purpose of obtaining immigration status.

PARTNER IN CANADA

An Open Work Permit May Be Available

A spouse, common-law partner or conjugal partner who is living in Canada and is being sponsored for permanent residence may be eligible for an open work permit.

Eligibility is separate from the permanent residence sponsorship itself and depends on the applicant meeting the current work permit requirements.

In many cases, the permanent residence application must already have been accepted for processing and an acknowledgement of receipt issued before the open work permit application can proceed.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Choose the Relationship Category Based on the Facts

Same-sex couples do not need to fit a special immigration category simply because of their sexual orientation.

The important question is whether the relationship meets Canada's requirements for spouses, common-law partners or conjugal partners.

In cases involving countries where same-sex relationships or marriage are restricted, the legal and practical barriers faced by the couple can be particularly important to the immigration assessment.
