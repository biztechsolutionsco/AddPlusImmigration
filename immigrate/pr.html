PERMANENT RESIDENCE

What Is Permanent Residence?

Permanent residence allows an individual to live, work and study in Canada on a long-term basis without being a Canadian citizen.

Permanent residents have many of the same rights and responsibilities as Canadian citizens, including access to many social benefits and the ability to live and work in Canada.

Canada offers several permanent residence pathways for skilled workers, provincial nominees, family members, caregivers and other eligible applicants.

The right pathway depends on your individual circumstances, including factors such as education, work experience, language ability, family ties, province of destination and current immigration status.

PATHWAYS

Common Permanent Residence Pathways

Canada has multiple immigration programs. The most suitable pathway depends on your personal and professional background.

01

Express Entry

A federal system used to manage applications for several economic immigration programs, including the Canadian Experience Class and Federal Skilled Worker Program.

02

Provincial Nominee Programs

Provinces and territories may nominate individuals who meet local labour market and immigration priorities.

03

Family Sponsorship

Eligible Canadian citizens and permanent residents may be able to sponsor certain family members for permanent residence.

04

Canadian Experience

Individuals with qualifying skilled work experience in Canada may have permanent residence options through economic programs.

05

Employer or Regional Pathways

Some permanent residence programs are linked to employers, communities, regions or specific labour market needs.

06

Other Immigration Programs

Additional federal or provincial pathways may be available depending on occupation, family circumstances, humanitarian considerations or program-specific criteria.

PERMANENT RESIDENT STATUS

Rights and Responsibilities of Permanent Residents

Permanent residence provides important rights in Canada, but it also comes with responsibilities.

01

Live in Canada

Permanent residents can live in Canada and establish their long-term home here.

02

Work in Canada

Permanent residents can generally work for employers across Canada without requiring a work permit.

03

Study in Canada

Permanent residents generally have access to Canadian education without requiring the same authorization as international students.

04

Access Eligible Benefits and Services

Permanent residents may have access to provincial health coverage and other government programs where eligible.

05

Maintain PR Status

Permanent residents must continue to meet applicable residency obligations in order to maintain their status.

06

Follow Canadian Law

Permanent residents must comply with Canadian laws and may face immigration consequences in certain circumstances.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Choosing the Right PR Pathway Matters

Permanent residence programs can differ significantly in their eligibility criteria, application procedures and selection systems.

An applicant may qualify for more than one pathway, while another applicant may need to improve language scores, gain additional work experience, obtain a provincial nomination or pursue another strategy before applying.

Because immigration programs and selection priorities can change, it is important to assess your current circumstances carefully.
