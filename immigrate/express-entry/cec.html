CANADIAN EXPERIENCE CLASS

What Is the Canadian Experience Class?

The Canadian Experience Class, commonly known as CEC, is one of the federal economic immigration programs managed through Canada's Express Entry system.

It is designed for skilled workers who have gained qualifying work experience in Canada and want to become permanent residents.

CEC can be an important pathway for eligible temporary foreign workers and former international students who later obtain qualifying Canadian work experience.

Meeting the minimum CEC requirements allows an eligible candidate to enter the Express Entry pool, but it does not guarantee an Invitation to Apply for permanent residence.

At a Glance

Work Experience At least 1 year in Canada within the last 3 years
Eligible Occupations NOC TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3
Language CLB/NCLC 7 for TEER 0–1; CLB/NCLC 5 for TEER 2–3
Job Offer Not required
Education No minimum education requirement

ELIGIBILITY

Key Canadian Experience Class Requirements

Applicants must satisfy the program's minimum requirements before they can qualify under CEC.

01

Canadian Work Experience

You generally need at least one year of qualifying skilled work experience gained in Canada during the three years before your application.

02

Skilled Occupation

Your qualifying Canadian work experience must be in an occupation classified under NOC TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3.

03

Authorized Work

The qualifying work experience must have been gained while you were authorized to work in Canada.

04

Language Ability

You must meet the required minimum English or French language level for the TEER level of your qualifying occupation.

05

Intention to Live Outside Quebec

The Canadian Experience Class is a federal immigration pathway for applicants intending to live outside Quebec.

06

Express Entry Eligibility

After meeting the CEC requirements, candidates must create an Express Entry profile and compete within the Express Entry selection system.

WORK EXPERIENCE

What Work Experience Can Count?

Not every period of employment in Canada can necessarily be used to meet the CEC work experience requirement.

✓

TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 Work

Qualifying work experience must be in an eligible skilled occupation under Canada's National Occupational Classification.

✓

Full-Time or Equivalent Part-Time

The required experience can generally be accumulated through full-time work or an equivalent amount of qualifying part-time work.

✓

More Than One Eligible Job

Qualifying Canadian experience may be accumulated through more than one eligible job, provided the applicable requirements are met.

✓

Recent Canadian Experience

The qualifying experience must fall within the required three-year period before the CEC application.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Work Experience That May Not Count

Correctly determining what work experience qualifies is an important part of a CEC assessment.

01

Work While a Full-Time Student

Canadian work experience gained while you were studying full-time does not count toward the minimum CEC work experience requirement.

02

Unauthorized Work

Work performed without the required authorization does not qualify as eligible Canadian work experience.

03

Ineligible Occupations

Work outside NOC TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 does not satisfy the CEC minimum skilled work experience requirement.

LANGUAGE REQUIREMENTS

Your Minimum Language Level Depends on Your Occupation

CEC applicants must demonstrate ability in speaking, listening, reading and writing in English or French using an approved language test.

For qualifying work experience in a TEER 0 or TEER 1 occupation, the minimum level is generally CLB 7 in English or NCLC 7 in French in each language ability.

For qualifying work experience in a TEER 2 or TEER 3 occupation, the minimum is generally CLB 5 in English or NCLC 5 in French in each language ability.

Approved Language Tests

Express Entry currently accepts designated English and French language tests. Your results must meet the applicable requirements and remain valid when required by IRCC.

ADDITIONAL FACTORS

Education and Job Offers

Neither education nor a job offer is required to meet the minimum CEC eligibility requirements, but other factors can still affect your Express Entry ranking.

✓

No Minimum Education Requirement

CEC does not have a minimum education requirement for program eligibility. However, qualifying education may contribute to your CRS score.

✓

Job Offer Not Required

You do not need a job offer to qualify for the Canadian Experience Class.

✓

CRS Still Matters

Once you enter the Express Entry pool, your profile is ranked using the Comprehensive Ranking System.

✓

Other Pathways May Be Available

Depending on your circumstances, you may also qualify for a provincial nomination or another permanent residence pathway.

APPLICATION PROCESS

How the CEC Process Works

Canadian Experience Class applications are managed through Express Entry.

1

Review Your Experience

Confirm that your Canadian work history meets the CEC requirements.

2

Complete Language Testing

Obtain valid test results that meet the minimum language level for your occupation.

3

Create Express Entry Profile

Submit your profile and enter the Express Entry pool if you meet the requirements.

4

Receive an Invitation

Eligible candidates may receive an Invitation to Apply in an Express Entry invitation round.

5

Apply for Permanent Residence

If invited, submit the complete permanent residence application and required supporting documents.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Canadian Work Experience Must Be Assessed Carefully

Having worked in Canada does not automatically mean that the experience qualifies for the Canadian Experience Class.

Your occupation, NOC classification, job duties, dates of employment, hours worked, authorization to work and whether you were studying can all be relevant when determining eligibility.

Your employment documentation should also accurately support the work experience claimed in your Express Entry profile and permanent residence application.
